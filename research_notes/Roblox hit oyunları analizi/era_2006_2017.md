# Classic-Era Roblox Hits (2006–2017): Catalog, Genres, Audience, Monetization

Method note: The Roblox fandom wiki returned HTTP 402 and Business Wire returned 403, so they could not be read. The most reliable data here is **primary data pulled from Roblox's own public APIs on 2026-11-08**:
- `apis.roblox.com/universes/v1/places/{placeId}/universe` → `games.roblox.com/v1/games?universeIds=…` gave the creator, place-creation date, total visits, live players and genre tag.
- `apis.roblox.com/game-passes/v1/universes/{id}/game-passes` gave the current gamepass list and prices.

Caveats on that data:
- The **"created" date is when the place was created, which is not always the public launch date.** Jailbreak is the clearest case: its place was created 2017-01-06 and it launched publicly on 2017-04-21.
- **Visit totals are cumulative to Nov 2026, not 2017 figures.**
- **Gamepass prices are current (2026), not 2017.**

Each game is cited by its Roblox game page `https://www.roblox.com/games/{placeId}`, which is the place the API data describes. Lines marked **(BK)** come from background knowledge that was not checked against a fetched source in this session. The report writer should treat them as unverified.

## Which games defined this era and when exactly did each break out?

### Takeaway
The era runs in three waves:
1. **2007–2009 "classic" foundations.** Examples are Crossroads, Sword Fights on the Heights, Work at a Pizza Place, Natural Disaster Survival, Apocalypse Rising and Lumber Tycoon 2.
2. **2012–2016 genre maturation.** Examples are Theme Park Tycoon 2, Murder Mystery 2, Prison Life, Bloxburg, Phantom Forces, Arsenal, Epic Minigames and MeepCity.
3. **The 2017 explosion.** Examples are Jailbreak, Royale High, Flood Escape 2, SharkBite, the launch of Adopt Me! and Mad City.

Jailbreak (launched April 2017; 103k CCU in Aug 2017; 121k in Dec 2017) and MeepCity (the first game to 1B visits) are the clearest breakout records of the window.

### Cited Findings

**Landmark records**
- Jailbreak public release: April 21, 2017. It earned over US$1M in its first year and had 8B plays as of Oct 2026 — [Wikipedia, List of Roblox games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Roblox_games).
- Jailbreak's place was created 2017-01-06 (creator Badimo). It had 8,088,144,088 visits and about 6.3k live players on 2026-11-08, genre tag "Town and City", 30-player servers — [Roblox API / game page](https://www.roblox.com/games/606849621).
- Jailbreak launch day CCU: ~75,000 simultaneous players, though a photo caption in the same article says 65,000 (internal inconsistency). It was expected to make "seven figures" in 2017. Co-creator Alex Balfanz was 18 and said earnings covered his Duke tuition — [Business Insider via Yahoo Finance, Jul 25 2017](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/video-game-youve-never-heard-130000452.html).
- Jailbreak peaked at 103,000 CCU on 13 Aug 2017, "the most concurrent gamers ever achieved by a single Roblox game" at that time — [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/503322-most-concurrent-players-for-a-videogame-made-in-roblox).
- Jailbreak passed 121k CCU on Dec 23, 2017, beating the record held by the Egg Hunt 2017 event. Sources also call it the first experience to reach 100k players. This was reported via a search snippet of the fandom page, which itself could not be fetched — [Roblox Wiki (snippet)](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Badimo/Jailbreak).
- Jailbreak won more than 10 awards at the 5th Bloxy Awards (Feb 17, 2018, honoring 2017 titles), including "Title of the Year" — [Business Wire press release (snippet; page 403)](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20180221005527/en/Roblox-Celebrates-the-Top-2017-Titles-With-5th-Annual-Bloxy-Awards).
- MeepCity was the first Roblox game to pass 1 billion total visits. It averaged 100,000 concurrent players in July 2018 — [Wikipedia, List of Roblox games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Roblox_games). The year it reached 1B is unconfirmed: only low-quality pages say 2017 ([Playgama](https://playgama.com/blog/game-faqs/what-was-the-first-roblox-game-to-reach-billion-visits/)).
- MeepCity's place was created 2016-02-23 by alexnewtron. It had 16,279,487,123 visits on 2026-11-08 and 40-player servers — [Roblox API / game page](https://www.roblox.com/games/370731277).
- Work at a Pizza Place was the first Roblox game played 100 million times and had 5B plays as of Feb 2025 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Roblox_games). Its place was created 2008-03-28 by Dued1, with 5,624,711,882 visits on 2026-11-08 — [Roblox API](https://www.roblox.com/games/192800).
- Business Insider (Jul 2017) named "Roblox High School", "Work At a Pizza Place" and "Pokémon Brick Bronze" alongside Jailbreak as notable games of mid-2017. It also profiled Miner's Haven (tycoon, Andrew Bereza, released June 2015) — [Yahoo Finance/BI](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/video-game-youve-never-heard-130000452.html).
- Business Insider coverage named MeepCity and Jailbreak as hits made by mostly-young independent developers — [search summary of BusinessInsider.in](https://www.businessinsider.in/This-video-game-is-turning-teenagers-into-millionaires-and-its-on-track-to-pay-out-70-million-more-this-year/articleshow/64978732.cms).

- Under the MAU figures available, peak CCU in the 2008–2014 period was far lower than 2017 numbers. Jailbreak's 103k was itself a platform record in Aug 2017, so no earlier game could have exceeded it.

## What genres dominated (tycoon, obby, roleplay, disaster survival, simulator) and why?

### Takeaway
Looking at both visits and Roblox's own genre tags, **town/life roleplay** is the dominant classic genre. That category includes Work at a Pizza Place, MeepCity, Bloxburg, RoCitizens, Royale High, Adopt Me!, Prison Life and Jailbreak. It is followed by **round-based party/survival** games (Natural Disaster Survival, MM2, Epic Minigames, Hide and Seek, SharkBite, Flood Escape), then **tycoons** (Lumber Tycoon 2, Theme Park Tycoon 2, Miner's Haven), **obbies** (Speed Run 4) and **FPS** (Phantom Forces, Arsenal, Counter Blox). "Simulators" were not yet a genre: their hits appear in 2018.

### Cited Findings
- Roblox genre tags returned by the API: 9 of the 26 queried titles were tagged "Town and City", including Pizza Place, MeepCity, Bloxburg, Jailbreak, Prison Life, RoCitizens and Mad City. Others were tagged Adventure, FPS, Fighting, Building, Horror and RPG — [Roblox games API, 2026-11-08](https://www.roblox.com/games/192800).
- MM2 is a social deduction game with randomly assigned roles each round, one of whom is the murderer — [exitlag](https://www.exitlag.com/blog/old-roblox-games-classic-favorites/).
- Its 32.16B lifetime visits make it the biggest era-launched title apart from Adopt Me! (45.36B) — [Roblox page MM2](https://www.roblox.com/games/142823291); [Adopt Me](https://www.roblox.com/games/920587237).
- Theme Park Tycoon 2 is compared to RollerCoaster Tycoon. Epic Minigames is inspired by Mario Party. Phantom Forces is compared to CoD, Battlefield and CS — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Roblox_games). This shows classic hits often **adapted known mainstream formats** to Roblox.
- Pokémon Brick Bronze was described as a "multiplayer Pokémon homage" — [BI/Yahoo 2017](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/video-game-youve-never-heard-130000452.html).
- The rising-water format dates to Flood Escape Classic, whose development began Sept 2011 — [Roblox listing](https://www.roblox.com/games/32990482).

### Inferences
- **Roleplay/town** games won on *social time*. Servers were relatively large (MeepCity 40, Jailbreak 30, per the API) and play had no fail state, which suits young players who mostly want to hang out with friends. The data also shows these titles accumulate the largest lifetime visit totals.
- **Round-based survival/party** games (NDS, MM2, Epic Minigames, SharkBite) have short sessions of a few minutes, simple rules and random roles or disasters. That design gives replayability without needing content updates. It suits the mobile and Xbox audience arriving in 2016–17.
- **Tycoons** used Roblox's "watch numbers go up" building loop. Their small servers (LT2 and TPT2 cap at 6 players, per the API) show they were more solo or creative experiences.
- **Cloned mainstream formats** were a major source of hits: RollerCoaster Tycoon, Mario Party, CoD/CS, Pokémon, DayZ (Apocalypse Rising), and cops-and-robbers / GTA-likes (Jailbreak, Ultimate Driving).

### Gaps
- No sourced genre-share statistic exists for 2017, such as the percentage of front-page games per genre.
- No sourced explanation from developers or Roblox of *why* each genre succeeded. The points above are inferences.

## What was the typical player age and how did it shape design?

### Takeaway
By 2017 Roblox was primarily a platform for under-13s and young teens. In Dec 2017, comScore ranked Roblox first in under-13 time spent. One 2017 article claims players were mostly 15–22 in 2015, which contradicts the general picture and should be flagged. Many top developers were themselves teenagers (Jailbreak and Miner's Haven creators were both 18 in 2017).

### Cited Findings
- comScore analysis for Dec 2017 ranked Roblox #1 in under-13 time spent at 51.5M hours, ahead of YouTube and Netflix. This was cited on Roblox's corporate blog, but the page now returns 404, so the citation comes from a search summary — [Roblox corporate blog](https://corporate.roblox.com/2018/03/roblox-emerges-top-online-entertainment-platform-kids-teens-2017).
- Engadget (Jul 2017) said "most players were between 15 and 22" in 2015. **Flag:** this conflicts with the under-13 picture, and Engadget may be referring to *creators* — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2017-07-22-roblox-30-million-pay-out-developers-2017-how.html).
- Roblox monthly players grew from 6M (Sept 2015) to 56M (Jul 2017), including 1.7M creators — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2017-07-22-roblox-30-million-pay-out-developers-2017-how.html).
- Another source puts MAU at 30M+ in Dec 2016 and 48M+ in Mar 2017; it is secondary and gives no primary citation — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/roblox-users).
- An aggregator gives under-13 share as 57.76% in 2018 and 54.86% in 2020. It is secondary, and no 2017 number was found — [Sidetrain](https://sidetrain.com/guides/roblox-user-demographic-statistics).
- Growth "accelerated with the smartphone, tablet, and Xbox versions" — [BI/Yahoo 2017](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/video-game-youve-never-heard-130000452.html).
- Adopt Me!'s user base is "younger than the rest of Roblox's on average", and younger players are especially susceptible to scams — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Roblox_games).
- MeepCity was briefly placed "under review" in Feb 2022 over online-dating and inappropriate content, after which its party features were removed — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Roblox_games). This later example of youth-safety pressure shows how social roleplay design interacts with a young audience.
- Developers were young: Jailbreak co-creator Alex Balfanz and Miner's Haven creator Andrew Bereza were both 18 in July 2017. Freelance artists cited in the same article were 17–18 — [BI/Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/video-game-youve-never-heard-130000452.html).

### Inferences
- Typical era player age: roughly **8–14**, with older teens in FPS and tycoon niches such as Phantom Forces, Apocalypse Rising and Lumber Tycoon 2. This estimate is triangulated from the comScore under-13 lead plus the 2018 aggregator (~58% under 13).
- That audience shaped design in several ways: simple rules, colorful town hubs, pets and dress-up (MeepCity, Royale High, Adopt Me!), short rounds (NDS, MM2), cartoon violence rather than gore, and a strong social and friend focus.
- Kid-made games for kids: teen developers built what they and their friends wanted to play, which helps explain the heavy roleplay and minigame bias.

### Gaps
- No primary 2017 age breakdown was found. Roblox S-1 (2021) data was not retrieved.
- No per-game age data exists, apart from the Adopt Me! "younger than average" note.

## How did these games make money?

### Takeaway
Classic hits were free to play and earned Robux from **gamepasses** (one-time permanent perks such as VIP, vehicles, bigger plots or extra roles), **developer products** (repeatable purchases such as currency, boosts or loot boxes) and occasionally **paid access** (Bloxburg, 25 Robux until 2024). Developers converted Robux to cash through **DevEx**. Platform payouts rose from $2.5M (2015) to $5.5M (2016) to about $30M projected for 2017. Top earners were projected at $2–3M in 2017.

### Cited Findings
- Developer payouts were $2.5M in 2015 and $5.5M in 2016, with about $30M projected for 2017. One developer was on track for about $3M, and two others for $2M each — [Roblox corporate, Jul 2017 (via search)](https://corporate.roblox.com/2017/07/roblox-track-pay-30-million-independent-game-developers-2017); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2017-07-22-roblox-30-million-pay-out-developers-2017-how.html).
- Forbes Mexico gives about $40M, which conflicts with the $30M figure — [Forbes MX](https://forbes.com.mx/?p=501007).

### Inferences
Four monetization archetypes are visible:
1. **VIP / status passes**, e.g. Jailbreak VIP, Pizza Place VIP and MeepCity PLUS.
2. **Role or vehicle unlocks** in roleplay games, e.g. Crime Boss, SWAT and Pizza Place houses and boats.
3. **Build-tool / quality-of-life passes** in tycoons and life sims, e.g. Bloxburg, TPT2 and LT2.
4. **Cosmetic and collectible economies** with trading, e.g. MM2 knives, Royale High outfits and Adopt Me! pets. This archetype is the strongest long-term earner.

Other monetization patterns:
- Classic 2008-era games (NDS, SFOTH, Crossroads) had little or no monetization. Monetization intensity grew sharply with DevEx-era (2013+) and 2016–17 titles.
- Paid access (Bloxburg) was rare but viable for a premium-quality life sim.
